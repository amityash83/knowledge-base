# Artificial Intelligence

## Summary
AI is the ability of machines to simulate human intelligence — learning, reasoning, decision-making, and pattern recognition. In short: machines that can learn and make decisions.

## Details

### Types of AI, by capability
- **Narrow AI (Weak AI)** — designed for a specific task, cannot generalize beyond training. Examples: chatbots, recommendation systems, image recognition. This is everything in production today.
- **General AI (AGI)** — human-level intelligence, able to perform any intellectual task. Not achieved yet.
- **Super AI** — beyond human intelligence. Hypothetical.

### Types of AI, by function
- **Reactive machines** — no memory, respond only to current input.
- **Limited memory** — uses past data; most deployed ML systems fall here.
- **Theory of mind** — understands emotions/intent (future).
- **Self-aware AI** — conscious systems (future, speculative).

### Core components of any AI system
1. **Data** — raw input: logs, text, images
2. **Algorithms** — rules/models that learn patterns
3. **Model** — the trained system
4. **Training** — learning from data
5. **Inference** — using the model in the real world
6. **Feedback loop** — improvement cycle
7. **Infrastructure** — compute (CPU/GPU), storage, pipelines (MLOps)

### Where AI shows up
- **Healthcare** — diagnosis, drug discovery
- **DevOps / AIOps** — log analysis, incident prediction, auto-remediation (see [[aiops]])
- **Finance** — fraud detection, risk analysis
- **E-commerce** — recommendations, chatbots

### AI vs ML vs DL
AI is the broad field; ML is the subset that learns from data; DL is the subset of ML using neural networks. See [[machine-learning-vs-deep-learning]] for the practical distinction.
